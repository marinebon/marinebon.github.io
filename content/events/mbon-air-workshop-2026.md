The Marine Biodiversity Observation Network (MBON) and the
[AIR Centre](https://www.aircentre.org/) are hosting a small, hands-on, interactive
workshop on **mobilising marine biological observation datasets to
[OBIS](https://obis.org/)**, held 19–23 October 2026 at the AIR Centre in Terceira
Island, Azores, Portugal. The five-day course is aimed at graduate students and
researchers — no previous knowledge of the tools presented is assumed.

The objective is to show data holders why it pays to follow good practices for
standardising biological data using widely accepted standards such as
[Darwin Core](https://dwc.tdwg.org/), across records of different biological
attributes and ecosystem observations from different sampling methodologies. By the
end of the workshop, each participant will have brought one of their own datasets to a
final maturity state aligned with best practices for data sharing and biodiversity
documentation — making marine biological data more available for scientific research,
species conservation, and ecosystem-based management.

A complementary **field day** takes participants to a rocky shore for training in the
collection and processing of benthic photo-quadrat imagery, and its analysis following
[MBON Pole to Pole](/network/pole-to-pole-americas/) monitoring
protocols, building capacity for long-term monitoring of rocky intertidal communities.

The five days cover:

1. **Data access from OBIS** — the OBIS Mapper and Portal, downloading records with R,
   and querying the OBIS API.
2. **Introduction to data standards and formatting** — Darwin Core and the data life
   cycle, plus planning for data collection.
3. **Fieldwork** — a full day on the rocky shore collecting photo-quadrat imagery.
4. **Photo-quadrat processing and dataset curation** — image analysis, dataset
   structuring and quality control, taxon matching against the
   [WoRMS](https://www.marinespecies.org/) Aphia ID match tool, OBIS QC checks, and
   eDNA data formats.
5. **Publishing your data in OBIS** — the OBIS IPT and the roles of OBIS Nodes,
   followed by conclusions and closure.

Participants are expected to be familiar with taxonomic occurrence data, spreadsheet
tools (Excel, LibreOffice, Google Sheets), handling CSV/TXT/Excel files, basic Python
or R programming, and basic data wrangling for marine biodiversity data — and to bring
a laptop with administrative privileges. Familiarity with sandy beach or rocky shore
environments is not mandatory.

The workshop is **in-person only**, with no virtual option and no recordings, as the
lessons build upon one another. The room is wheelchair and scooter accessible, and
accessible restrooms are available.

Workshop registration is closed. 
The AIR Centre also funded researchers from institutions along the West Atlantic coast of Africa to attend. The
full [schedule and setup instructions](https://marinebon.org/mbon-air-workshop-2026/)
remain public, as do the reference materials linked from each day's session, for anyone
who wants to follow the curriculum or reuse the exercises. For more information,
contact [joana.soares@aircentre.org](mailto:joana.soares@aircentre.org).
