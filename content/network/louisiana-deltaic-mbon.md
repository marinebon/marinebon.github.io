The **Louisiana Deltaic Estuaries MBON** (LA MBON) — *Sea Level Rise Sentinels* —
is the first U.S. MBON project sited in an estuarine system. Coastal Louisiana's highly productive and diverse
estuaries make it a globally significant deltaic coast and a vital sea-level-rise
sentinel: it experiences some of the highest rates of coastal land loss in the
world, mirroring the range of responses that estuaries elsewhere may face as seas
continue to rise.

Led by Cassandra Glaspie at Louisiana State University and established in 2023, the
project collates and synthesizes data from existing monitoring programs to make them
more accessible and comparable, and works toward efficient methods for observing
estuarine biodiversity. It pairs traditional sampling — benthic cores and taxonomic
identification of macrofauna — with emerging approaches including environmental DNA
(eDNA), remote sensing, and passive acoustics, validating the new methods against the
traditional ones. Seasonal sampling spans long-term monitoring locations in Barataria
Bay, Terrebonne Bay, and Atchafalaya Bay.

LA MBON publishes its biodiversity observations through the MBON data portal and
[OBIS](/datasets/?q=LA%20MBON), including benthic core macrofauna and oyster-reef
associated biodiversity datasets.
